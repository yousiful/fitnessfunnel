If you are over 40 and have never lifted weights, or you stopped years ago, you are in good company. Most people starting at this age want the same things: feel stronger, move without aches, keep up with kids or grandkids, and stay independent for a long time.

Strength training helps with all of that, and you can start at home with very little equipment. Here is why it matters more after 40, a simple plan with sets and reps, and how to progress without getting hurt.

## Why strength training matters after 40

Harvard Health Publishing reports that after age 30, people who are physically inactive can lose about 3 to 5 percent of their muscle mass per decade. That slow loss makes everyday things harder over time, like climbing stairs, carrying bags, or getting up from the floor.

Regular strength training slows that decline and can rebuild strength at any age. It also puts healthy stress on bones, helps with balance, and makes daily movement easier.

The CDC recommends that adults do **muscle-strengthening activities on at least two days a week** that work all the major muscle groups: legs, hips, back, chest, shoulders, arms, and core. That is alongside 150 minutes a week of moderate activity like brisk walking.

## What changes after 40 (and what to do about it)

- **Recovery takes a bit longer.** Leave at least one day between strength sessions for the same muscles. Two or three full-body workouts a week is plenty.
- **Joints like a longer warm-up.** Five to eight minutes of easy movement before you lift makes a big difference.
- **Form counts more than weight.** Most injuries come from rushing or lifting too heavy too soon. Slow, controlled reps are safer and work the muscle well.
- **Sleep and protein matter more.** Muscle is built during recovery, so eating enough protein and sleeping well are part of the training.

## What you need

- A pair of adjustable dumbbells, or two or three pairs of fixed ones (many beginners start with 5 to 15 lb for women and 10 to 25 lb for men, but go by feel)
- A resistance band with handles or a loop band
- A sturdy chair or bench
- A mat or carpet for floor work

Bodyweight alone is enough for the first few weeks if you do not have weights yet.

## The warm-up (5 to 8 minutes)

1. March in place or walk around the house: 1 minute
2. Arm circles: 10 forward, 10 back
3. Hip hinges with hands on hips: 10 reps
4. Bodyweight squats to a chair: 10 reps
5. Wall push-ups: 10 reps
6. Glute bridges: 10 reps

## The beginner full-body workout

Do this 2 or 3 days a week, for example Monday, Wednesday, and Friday. Rest 60 to 90 seconds between sets. Start with 2 sets of each move and work up to 3.

### 1. Goblet squat
2 to 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps.
Hold one dumbbell at your chest, feet a little wider than hips. Sit your hips back and down as if sitting in a chair, chest up, knees tracking over your toes. Stand by pushing through your whole foot.
**Easier:** squat to a chair and stand back up. **Harder:** add a three-second lowering phase.

### 2. Dumbbell Romanian deadlift
2 to 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps.
Stand tall with dumbbells in front of your thighs. With a slight knee bend, push your hips back and slide the weights down your legs until you feel a stretch in your hamstrings, keeping your back flat. Squeeze your glutes to stand up.
**Easier:** practice the hinge with no weight, hands on hips. **Harder:** heavier dumbbells or a single-leg version holding a counter.

### 3. Push-up (to your level)
2 to 3 sets of 6 to 12 reps.
Start on a wall, kitchen counter, or bench. Hands slightly wider than shoulders, body in a straight line. Lower your chest, then press away.
**Easier:** wall push-up. **Harder:** knees on the floor, then full push-ups on your toes.

### 4. One-arm dumbbell row
2 to 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps per side.
One hand and knee on a bench or chair, back flat. Pull the dumbbell toward your hip and squeeze your shoulder blade back. Lower slowly.
**Easier:** band row anchored in a door. **Harder:** heavier weight with a one-second pause at the top.

### 5. Reverse lunge
2 sets of 8 to 10 reps per leg.
Step one foot back and lower until both knees are bent around 90 degrees, front knee over the ankle. Push through the front heel to return.
**Easier:** hold a chair for balance, or swap for step-ups on a low stair. **Harder:** hold dumbbells at your sides.

### 6. Dumbbell overhead press
2 sets of 8 to 12 reps.
Seated or standing, dumbbells at shoulder height. Press overhead without arching your lower back. Lower slowly.
**Easier:** one arm at a time. **Harder:** standing, with a slightly heavier weight.

### 7. Plank
2 to 3 holds of 20 to 40 seconds.
Forearms on the floor, body in a straight line, belly braced, glutes tight.
**Easier:** on your knees or with hands on a counter. **Harder:** add 10 seconds each week.

## How to progress

The key idea is progressive overload: doing a little more over time. Pick a weight where the last two reps of each set feel hard but your form stays clean. When you can hit the top of the rep range for all sets in two workouts in a row, add a little weight (often 2.5 to 5 lb) or a few reps.

A simple 8-week outline:

| Weeks | Sets per exercise | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | 2 | Learn the moves, light weights, slow reps |
| 3 to 4 | 2 to 3 | Add a set, small weight increases |
| 5 to 6 | 3 | Push closer to the top of the rep range |
| 7 to 8 | 3 | Add weight where form allows, then take an easier week |

## Is it different for women and men over 40?

The basics are the same for everyone: the same movements, the same rep ranges, and the same rules about progressing slowly. Women going through perimenopause or menopause have extra reason to prioritize strength work because of changes in bone and muscle. Our guide to [workouts for women over 50](academy:workouts-for-women-over-50) covers that in more detail, including balance work. Men over 40 often do best by holding back their ego on weight and focusing on full range of motion.

## Recovery: protein and sleep

Muscle repairs and grows while you rest. Two things help most:

- **Protein:** most people who strength train do well somewhere above the basic RDA of 0.8 grams per kilogram of body weight. Our guide on [how much protein to eat a day](academy:how-much-protein-per-day) shows how to work out your number.
- **Sleep:** the CDC recommends at least 7 hours a night for adults. See our guide on [sleep and muscle recovery](academy:sleep-and-muscle-recovery) for simple ways to sleep better.

## Staying safe

Check with your doctor before starting, especially if you have heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, joint problems, osteoporosis, or a past injury. Muscle soreness for a day or two after training is normal, especially early on. Sharp pain, joint pain that gets worse during a set, dizziness, or chest discomfort are signs to stop right away and get checked.

Breathe steadily through each rep. Breathe out as you push or lift, and in as you lower. Avoid holding your breath while straining, which raises blood pressure.

## Sticking with it

The best program is the one you will still be doing in six months. Two 30-minute sessions a week done consistently will do more for you than an ambitious plan you drop after three weeks.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it too late to start strength training after 40?
No. Muscle and strength respond to training at any age, including well into your 60s, 70s, and beyond. Start light, focus on form, and build up slowly.

### How often should you strength train over 40?
The CDC recommends muscle-strengthening activity at least two days a week. For most beginners over 40, two or three full-body sessions a week with a rest day in between works well.

### Can you build muscle after 40?
Yes. Lifting progressively heavier weights, eating enough protein, and getting good sleep will build muscle at 40 and beyond. Progress may be a little slower than in your 20s, but it still happens.

### Can I do strength training at home over 40?
Yes. A pair of dumbbells, a resistance band, and a chair cover every major muscle group. Squats, hinges, push-ups, rows, lunges, presses, and planks make a full workout.

### Should I lift heavy or light weights after 40?
Choose a weight that makes the last two reps of each set hard while your form stays clean. For most beginners that means moderate weights for 8 to 12 reps, increased slowly over weeks.
