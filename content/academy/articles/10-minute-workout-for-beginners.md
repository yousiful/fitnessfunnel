Ten minutes is enough to get your heart rate up, work every major muscle group, and build a habit you can keep. It's short enough to fit before work, during nap time, or while dinner is in the oven. This guide gives you a full 10-minute routine you can do in your living room with no equipment, plus how to adjust it and how to build on it over time.

If you're new to exercise, over 60, pregnant or postpartum, or have joint, heart, or blood pressure concerns, check with your doctor before starting. Stop any move that causes sharp pain.

## Is a 10-minute workout enough?

For a beginner, yes, it's a very good place to start. The CDC recommends adults get 150 minutes of moderate activity a week plus muscle-strengthening activity on at least 2 days. The WHO notes that any amount of activity is better than none. A 10-minute session counts toward your weekly total, and doing it most days adds up to 50 to 70 minutes a week before you add any walking.

A short routine won't build fitness as fast as longer, progressively harder training. Its real strength is that you'll actually do it. Once 10 minutes feels easy, you can do it twice, add a walk, or move up to our [20-minute beginner workout at home](academy:beginner-workout-at-home-no-equipment).

## How the routine works

- **Total time:** 10 minutes
- **Warm-up:** 2 minutes
- **Main circuit:** 6 moves, 25 seconds of work and 10 seconds of rest, done twice (7 minutes)
- **Cool-down:** 1 minute

Set a timer app to 25 seconds on and 10 seconds off, or watch a clock. Move at a pace where you're breathing harder but could still say a short sentence.

## Warm-up (2 minutes)

1. **March in place (45 seconds).** Swing your arms and lift your knees comfortably.
2. **Arm circles (30 seconds).** Small circles forward, then backward, gradually getting bigger.
3. **Hip circles (15 seconds each way).** Hands on hips, draw slow circles.
4. **Slow half squats (15 seconds).** Sit back a few inches and stand up.

## The 6 moves

### 1. Chair squat (25 seconds)
Stand in front of a sturdy chair, feet hip-width apart. Sit back until you lightly touch the seat, then stand up tall.
- **Form cue:** Weight in your whole foot, knees tracking over your toes.
- **Easier:** Sit down fully and use your hands to help you up.
- **Harder:** Don't touch the chair, or squat without one.

### 2. Wall push-up (25 seconds)
Hands on a wall at chest height, feet back so your body leans at an angle. Bend your elbows, bring your chest toward the wall, push back.
- **Form cue:** Keep a straight line from head to heels.
- **Easier:** Step closer to the wall.
- **Harder:** Use a kitchen counter or the floor on your knees.

### 3. Step-back lunge with support (25 seconds, alternate legs)
Hold the back of a chair. Step one foot back, bend both knees a little, then return.
- **Easier:** Small steps and shallow bends.
- **Harder:** Go deeper, or let go of the chair.

### 4. Standing knee drive (25 seconds)
Stand tall with arms overhead. Pull one knee up as you bring your hands down toward it, then switch sides. This is a cardio move without jumping.
- **Easier:** Slower pace, lower knees.
- **Harder:** Speed up while keeping control.

### 5. Glute bridge (25 seconds)
Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Press through your heels to lift your hips, squeeze your glutes at the top, lower slowly.
- **Easier:** Smaller lift.
- **Harder:** Hold each rep at the top for 2 seconds.

### 6. Dead bug (25 seconds)
Lie on your back with arms reaching to the ceiling and knees bent at 90 degrees above your hips. Slowly lower your right arm and left leg toward the floor, return, then switch.
- **Form cue:** Keep your lower back gently pressed into the floor.
- **Easier:** Move only your legs, one at a time, with feet tapping the floor.
- **Harder:** Move slower and reach farther.

Rest 10 seconds after each move. After all six, start the circuit again for a second round.

## Cool-down (1 minute)

Walk around the room slowly for 20 seconds, then hold a standing thigh stretch and a hamstring stretch for 20 seconds on each side. Breathe slowly.

## 10-minute workout summary

| Part | What to do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | March, arm circles, hip circles, half squats | 2 min |
| Round 1 | 6 moves, 25 sec on, 10 sec off | 3.5 min |
| Round 2 | Repeat round 1 | 3.5 min |
| Cool-down | Slow walk and stretches | 1 min |

## How to make 10 minutes count

- **Keep rest short.** Ten seconds goes fast, and short rests keep your heart rate up.
- **Focus on form first.** Smooth reps build strength and protect your joints. Speed can come later.
- **Progress every week or two.** Move one exercise to its harder version, or change the timing to 30 seconds on and 10 seconds off.
- **Do it at the same time each day.** Attaching it to something you already do, like after your morning coffee, makes it automatic.
- **Track your sessions.** Ticking off each workout keeps you honest and shows progress. Our [free workout tracker](/app/) logs workouts and streaks so you can watch the habit build.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a 10-minute workout be effective?
Yes. Ten minutes of steady effort works your muscles and raises your heart rate, and it counts toward the CDC's 150 minutes of moderate activity a week. For beginners, a short workout you do consistently is far more useful than a long one you avoid.

### Can you do a 10-minute workout every day?
Most beginners can do a gentle 10-minute bodyweight routine most days, especially if it's moderate in effort. If you feel very sore or tired, swap in a walk or a rest day. Mixing strength days with walking days gives your muscles time to recover.

### Can 10 minutes of exercise a day help you lose weight?
It can help as one part of a bigger picture. Exercise burns some calories and builds strength, but weight loss depends mostly on eating habits over time. Pair short workouts with balanced meals, more daily walking, and good sleep, and talk to your doctor if you have a lot of weight to lose.

### Is a 10-minute workout a day enough for beginners?
It's a solid starting point. Over time, aim to build up toward 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, which you can reach by adding walks or doing your 10-minute routine twice on some days.

### What is an easy 10-minute workout for beginners?
A routine of chair squats, wall push-ups, supported lunges, standing knee drives, glute bridges, and dead bugs, done for 25 seconds each with short rests, is easy to learn and needs no equipment. Use the easier option for any move that feels too hard.
