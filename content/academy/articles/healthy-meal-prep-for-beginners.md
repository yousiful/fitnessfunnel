Meal prep has a reputation for being 21 identical containers of chicken and broccoli. You do not have to do it that way. For a beginner, meal prep means doing a little cooking ahead so that on a tired Tuesday, a healthy meal is faster than ordering takeout.

This guide gives you a simple method, a shopping list, a 90-minute plan, and the food safety rules you need so nothing in your fridge goes bad.

## Start small

Most people quit meal prep because they try to cook every meal for the whole week on their first Sunday. It takes four hours, the food gets boring by Wednesday, and they never do it again.

Start with one meal. For most people that is lunch, because it is the meal most often grabbed on the go. Prep four or five lunches. Once that feels easy, add breakfasts, then a couple of dinners.

## The building block method

Instead of following a rigid recipe, cook a few separate parts and mix them through the week. Each meal is one item from each row:

| Building block | Easy options |
|---|---|
| Protein | Chicken thighs or breasts, ground turkey, hard-boiled eggs, canned tuna, tofu, lentils, black beans |
| Carb | Brown rice, quinoa, roasted potatoes or sweet potatoes, whole wheat wraps, pasta |
| Vegetables | Roasted broccoli, peppers, zucchini, green beans, bagged salad, frozen mixed veg |
| Flavor | Salsa, pesto, teriyaki sauce, hummus, a lemon and olive oil dressing, hot sauce |

Monday might be chicken, rice, broccoli, and teriyaki. Wednesday is the same chicken in a wrap with salad and hummus. Same cooking, two different-tasting lunches.

A palm-sized portion of protein at each meal is a good habit. If you want a number, our guide on [how much protein to eat a day](academy:how-much-protein-per-day) walks you through working out yours.

## Your starter shopping list

For five lunches and five breakfasts:

- **Protein:** 2 lb chicken (thighs stay juicier than breasts), 1 dozen eggs, 2 cans of beans or lentils
- **Carbs:** 1 bag of brown rice or quinoa, 2 lb sweet potatoes, rolled oats
- **Vegetables:** 2 heads of broccoli, 3 bell peppers, 1 bag of spinach or salad, 1 bag of frozen mixed vegetables
- **Breakfast extras:** plain Greek yogurt, frozen berries, a jar of peanut butter
- **Flavor:** one or two sauces you already like, olive oil, salt, pepper, garlic powder

## Containers

You do not need a fancy set. Get 8 to 10 containers of the same size so they stack, ideally glass with snap lids. Glass reheats well in the microwave and does not hold onto smells. A few small containers for sauces stop your food from going soggy.

## The 90-minute Sunday plan

1. **Minute 0:** Preheat the oven to 425°F. Start the rice or quinoa on the stove.
2. **Minute 5:** Toss the chicken with oil, salt, pepper, and garlic powder. Put it on a sheet pan.
3. **Minute 10:** Cube the sweet potatoes, toss with oil and salt, and put them on a second sheet pan. Both pans go in the oven.
4. **Minute 15:** Put 6 eggs in a pot of cold water, bring to a boil, cover, turn off the heat, and leave for 10 to 12 minutes. Move them to cold water after.
5. **Minute 20:** Chop the broccoli and peppers. They go on a third pan once there is room, or roast them after the potatoes come out (about 20 minutes).
6. **Minute 30:** Make five jars of overnight oats: 1/2 cup oats, 1/2 cup milk, 1/4 cup Greek yogurt, and berries in each.
7. **Minute 40:** Check the chicken. It is done when the thickest part reads 165°F on a meat thermometer. Sweet potatoes are done when a fork slides in easily.
8. **Minute 60:** Let everything cool for a few minutes, then portion into containers.
9. **Minute 75:** Into the fridge. Wash up. Done.

Put a podcast on and it goes faster than you would think.

## Food safety basics

These rules come from the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service:

- **Refrigerate within 2 hours** of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is above 90°F.
- **Keep your fridge at 40°F or below**, and the freezer at 0°F.
- **Eat cooked leftovers within 3 to 4 days.** That is why prepping four days ahead is the sweet spot.
- **Reheat to 165°F**, and stir halfway through when using the microwave so there are no cold spots.
- **Freeze what you will not eat by day 4.** Soups, chili, cooked grains, and cooked chicken freeze well.
- **Thaw in the fridge**, not on the counter.

A simple trick is to prep Sunday for Monday through Wednesday, then do a quick 30-minute top-up on Wednesday night for the rest of the week. Or freeze two portions on Sunday and move them to the fridge the night before.

## Meals that hold up well

Some foods are great on day four. Others get sad by day two.

**Hold up well:** roasted vegetables, rice and grains, chili and soups, curries, sheet pan chicken, hard-boiled eggs (peeled or not), overnight oats, burrito bowls, pasta salad with oil-based dressing.

**Do not hold up well:** dressed salads (keep dressing separate), fried or breaded foods, avocado once cut, delicate fish after day two, anything with a cream sauce you plan to freeze.

## Five easy beginner meals

1. **Burrito bowl:** rice, ground turkey with taco seasoning, black beans, peppers, salsa.
2. **Sheet pan chicken and veg:** chicken thighs, broccoli, sweet potato, any sauce.
3. **Big batch chili:** ground beef or turkey, two cans of beans, canned tomatoes, onion, chili powder. Makes six portions and freezes well.
4. **Egg muffins:** whisk 10 eggs with spinach, peppers, and a little cheese, pour into a muffin tin, bake at 350°F for about 20 minutes. Grab two for breakfast.
5. **Lentil curry:** red lentils, a jar of curry sauce, spinach, served over rice. Cheap, filling, and vegetarian.

## Making it stick

Pick a set day and time and treat it like an appointment. Sunday afternoon works for most people, but any day you are home is fine. Keep a short list on your phone of meals you liked, so you are not starting from scratch each week.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start meal prepping as a beginner?
Pick one meal, usually lunch, and prep four or five of them on one day. Cook a protein, a carb, and a vegetable separately, then mix and match with different sauces. Add more meals once that feels easy.

### How long does meal prep last in the fridge?
The USDA recommends eating cooked leftovers within 3 to 4 days if your fridge is at 40°F or below. Freeze anything you will not get to by then.

### What are the easiest healthy meal prep ideas?
Burrito bowls, sheet pan chicken with roasted vegetables, big batch chili, egg muffins, overnight oats, and lentil curry are all simple and hold up well for several days.

### How do I meal prep for the week without food going bad?
Prep for three or four days at a time, then do a short top-up midweek or freeze a few portions and thaw them in the fridge the night before. Keep dressings and sauces in separate small containers so food does not go soggy.

### Is meal prepping good for weight loss?
It can help, because planned portions make it easier to eat consistently and skip last-minute takeout. Weight loss still comes down to your overall eating and activity, so check with your doctor before starting a weight loss plan.
