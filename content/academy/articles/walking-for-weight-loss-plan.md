Walking is one of the easiest ways to start moving more. You already know how to do it, it costs nothing, and it's gentle on most joints. With a plan, it also becomes something you can build on week after week instead of a walk you take when you remember.

Below is a free 8-week walking schedule, how fast to walk, how to think about steps, and how to pair walking with eating habits, since food matters a lot for weight loss too.

If you have a heart condition, joint pain, diabetes, are pregnant or postpartum, or haven't exercised in a long time, check with your doctor before you start a new walking plan, especially one aimed at weight loss.

## How walking helps with weight loss

Walking burns extra energy, and over weeks that adds up. On its own, though, it usually burns less than people expect, so it works best as one part of a plan that also includes eating habits you can keep. Walking also helps in quieter ways. It gets you outside, helps many people sleep and feel better, and builds a daily habit that makes other healthy changes easier.

The CDC recommends at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week for adults, and notes that some people need more than that to lose weight or keep it off. Brisk walking counts as moderate activity. The plan below gets you to 150 minutes by week 4 and past it after that.

## How fast should you walk?

Aim for a brisk pace. Use the talk test, which the CDC suggests: you can talk in sentences, but you couldn't sing. You should feel warm and breathe a bit harder than normal.

On easy days, a comfortable pace is fine. Every step counts toward your total.

## How many steps a day?

10,000 steps is a popular number, but it isn't a medical rule. A better approach is to start from where you are now:

1. Wear a phone or watch for 3 normal days and note your average daily steps.
2. Add about 1,000 steps a day to that number each week or two.
3. Keep going until you're at a level that feels sustainable, whether that's 7,000, 8,000, or 10,000.

If counting steps stresses you out, count minutes instead. The plan below uses minutes.

## The 8-week walking for weight loss plan

Walk 5 days a week. Take 2 rest days, or use them for gentle stretching or strength work. "Brisk" means talk-test pace. "Easy" means comfortable.

| Week | Walks per week | Each walk | Weekly total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | 15 minutes easy | 75 minutes |
| 2 | 5 | 20 minutes (5 easy, 10 brisk, 5 easy) | 100 minutes |
| 3 | 5 | 25 minutes (5 easy, 15 brisk, 5 easy) | 125 minutes |
| 4 | 5 | 30 minutes (5 easy, 20 brisk, 5 easy) | 150 minutes |
| 5 | 5 | 30 minutes, add 2 hill or interval walks | 150 minutes |
| 6 | 5 | 35 minutes, 2 interval walks | 175 minutes |
| 7 | 5 | 40 minutes, 2 interval walks | 200 minutes |
| 8 | 5 | 40-45 minutes, 1 longer weekend walk of 60 minutes | 220+ minutes |

If a week feels too hard, repeat it. Taking 10 or 12 weeks to finish is completely fine.

### What an interval walk looks like
After a 5-minute easy warm-up, walk fast for 1 minute, then easy for 2 minutes. Repeat 6 to 8 times, then cool down with 5 easy minutes. Fast should feel like you're running late for something, not racing.

### What a hill walk looks like
Pick a route with a gentle hill or use a treadmill incline of 3 to 6 percent. Walk up at a steady pace and take the downhill easy. If you have sore knees, go easy on the downhill sections or skip hills for now.

## Add two short strength days

Walking works your legs and heart, but the CDC also recommends muscle-strengthening activities on two or more days a week. Ten to fifteen minutes is enough to start: chair squats, wall push-ups, glute bridges, and standing calf raises. ## Pair walking with food habits you can keep

Walking helps, but what you eat usually matters more for weight loss. You don't need a strict diet. Pick one or two of these and practice them for a few weeks before adding more:

- **Build meals around protein and vegetables.** Protein helps you feel full. This guide on [how much protein per day](academy:how-much-protein-per-day) gives you a simple target.
- **Drink water first.** Many people drink a lot of calories without noticing, from soda, sweet coffee drinks, and juice.
- **Serve smaller portions** and go back for more if you're still hungry.
- **Plan a few easy meals** so you're not deciding when you're tired and hungry.

## Tips that make walking stick

- **Same time, most days.** Morning before work, after lunch, or after dinner. A set time turns walking into a habit.
- **Good shoes.** Comfortable, supportive walking shoes make a bigger difference than any gadget.
- **Walk with someone.** A friend, partner, or dog makes it harder to skip.
- **Track it.** Log each walk in the [free workout tracker](/app/) and watch your streak grow. On days you don't feel like it, a 10-minute walk still keeps the streak alive.
- **Have a bad-weather backup.** A mall, a treadmill, or walking laps around your home all work.
- **Watch the scale less.** Weight goes up and down from day to day. Look at the trend over several weeks, and notice other changes like how your clothes fit, your energy, and how far you can walk.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should I walk a day to lose weight?
For most adults, 30 to 45 minutes of brisk walking on most days is a good goal. The CDC recommends at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, and some people need more to lose weight. Start shorter and build up, and pair walking with eating changes for the best chance of progress.

### How far should I walk daily for weight loss?
Distance depends on your pace, but 30 minutes of brisk walking covers roughly 1.5 to 2 miles for many people. Focus on minutes and effort first. Distance tends to increase on its own as you get fitter.

### Is walking enough to lose weight?
Walking can help, but most people also need to adjust what and how much they eat. Walking plus a few steady food habits works better than either one alone. Results vary from person to person, so check with your doctor about what's realistic for you.

### Which walk is best for weight loss?
Brisk walking at a talk-test pace is a good base. Adding interval walks and hill or incline walks a couple of times a week raises the effort without needing to run. The best walk is the one you'll do consistently.

### Is treadmill walking as good as walking outside?
Both count. A treadmill makes it easy to control pace and incline and is handy in bad weather. Outdoor walking gives you fresh air and varied ground. Use whichever one gets you moving more often.
