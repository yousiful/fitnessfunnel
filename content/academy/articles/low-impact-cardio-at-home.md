Low impact cardio means one foot stays on the floor at all times. There is no jumping, no hard landing, and no pounding through your knees, hips, or back. Your heart still works, your breathing still picks up, and you can do all of it in a living room with the TV on.

This guide gives you 10 moves, a 20-minute routine you can start today, ways to make it easier or harder, and a simple week to follow. No equipment needed. A sturdy chair helps.

If you have joint pain, a heart condition, are pregnant or postpartum, or have been inactive for a long time, check with your doctor before starting a new routine. If anything hurts sharply during a move, stop.

## How hard should low impact cardio feel?

Use the talk test, which the CDC recommends for judging effort. At a moderate pace you can talk in full sentences but you can't sing. If you can sing, move a little faster or make your arms bigger. If you can only get out a word or two, slow down.

The CDC recommends adults get at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, plus two days of muscle-strengthening work. That sounds like a lot until you break it up. Thirty minutes, five days a week, gets you there. So does 20 minutes a day, every day, plus a walk on the weekend.

## 10 low impact cardio moves you can do at home

Do each move for the time listed in the routine below. Form cues first, then the easier and harder versions.

### 1. March in place
Stand tall, lift one knee at a time to about hip height, and swing your arms. Land softly through the whole foot.
- Easier: smaller knee lifts, hands on a chair back.
- Harder: lift knees higher and pump your arms overhead.

### 2. Step touch
Step your right foot out to the side, bring your left foot in to meet it, then go the other way. Keep a slight bend in your knees.
- Easier: smaller steps, arms at your sides.
- Harder: take two steps each way and add a big arm swing.

### 3. Heel digs
Tap one heel forward on the floor while you bend the standing knee slightly, then switch. Punch your arms forward as you go.
- Easier: slower pace, no arms.
- Harder: speed up and press your arms overhead each rep.

### 4. Standing knee drives
Reach both arms up, then pull them down as you drive one knee up. Alternate sides or do 30 seconds per side.
- Easier: hold a chair with one hand.
- Harder: add a slight twist, bringing the opposite elbow toward the knee.

### 5. Side reach and tap
Step wide to one side and reach the opposite arm over your head, like you're grabbing something off a high shelf. Switch sides.
- Easier: shorter step, smaller reach.
- Harder: sink a little lower into the step.

### 6. Hamstring curls
Step out to one side and curl the other heel toward your glutes. Pull your elbows back as the heel comes up.
- Easier: no step, curl in place.
- Harder: move faster and keep a steady rhythm.

### 7. Low step-ups
Use the bottom stair. Step up with the right foot, bring the left up, step down right, then left. Switch the lead leg halfway through.
- Easier: hold the railing and go slowly.
- Harder: add a knee lift at the top.

### 8. Boxer shuffle with punches
Stand in a staggered stance and shift your weight side to side on light feet while you throw straight punches.
- Easier: plant your feet and punch.
- Harder: faster punches and quicker weight shifts.

### 9. Speed skater (no hop)
Step back and diagonal with one leg, like a curtsy, and swing your arms across your body. Step back to center and switch.
- Easier: shallow step, hand on a chair.
- Harder: go a little deeper and touch the floor with the opposite hand if your back allows.

### 10. Seated march
Sit near the front of a chair, sit tall, and march your knees up while you swing your arms. This one is great on days your legs or knees are tired. For more ideas, see these [chair exercises for seniors](academy:chair-exercises-for-seniors), which work for any age.
- Easier: slower pace.
- Harder: add overhead arm presses.

## The 20-minute low impact cardio routine

Do each move for 45 seconds, then rest or march slowly for 15 seconds. That's one round of 10 minutes. Do two rounds.

| Minute | Move |
|---|---|
| 0-1 | March in place (warm-up pace) |
| 1-2 | Step touch |
| 2-3 | Heel digs |
| 3-4 | Standing knee drives |
| 4-5 | Side reach and tap |
| 5-6 | Hamstring curls |
| 6-7 | Low step-ups |
| 7-8 | Boxer shuffle with punches |
| 8-9 | Speed skater (no hop) |
| 9-10 | Seated march |

Repeat once for 20 minutes. Finish with 2 minutes of slow marching and a few easy stretches for your calves, thighs, and chest.

**If 20 minutes is too much right now**, do one round. Ten minutes counts. Add the second round when the first one feels manageable, maybe after a week or two.

**When it starts to feel easy**, try one of these, one at a time:
- Work 50 seconds, rest 10.
- Add a third round.
- Use the harder version of every move.
- Hold light dumbbells or water bottles for the arm movements.

## A sample week

This week mixes cardio, strength, and rest, so your body has time to adapt.

| Day | What to do |
|---|---|
| Monday | 20-minute low impact cardio routine |
| Tuesday | 20-30 minute walk |
| Wednesday | Strength day (bodyweight squats to a chair, wall push-ups, glute bridges) |
| Thursday | 20-minute low impact cardio routine |
| Friday | 20-30 minute walk |
| Saturday | Strength day or a longer walk |
| Sunday | Rest or gentle stretching |

That lands you around 150 minutes of moderate activity for the week, with two strength days. If walking is your favorite part, this [walking for weight loss plan](academy:walking-for-weight-loss-plan) builds it up week by week.

## Low impact cardio for sore knees

Most of these moves are friendly to sensitive knees, but a few need care. Keep step-ups low, keep the speed skater shallow, and skip any move that causes sharp or growing pain. Seated marches and heel digs are usually the gentlest. If knees are your main concern, read these [exercises for bad knees at home](academy:exercises-for-bad-knees), which focus on strengthening the muscles that support the joint.

A good rule from physical therapy circles: some mild discomfort is fine, but pain that gets worse during the workout or is still worse the next day means you should scale back or swap the move.

## Common mistakes

- **Going too hard on day one.** Start at a pace you could repeat tomorrow.
- **Locking your knees.** Keep a soft bend in the standing leg.
- **Holding your breath.** Breathe steadily and keep talking if you can.
- **Skipping the warm-up.** Two easy minutes of marching gets your joints ready.
- **Wearing socks on a hard floor.** Supportive shoes help with grip and comfort.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best low impact cardio to do at home?
Walking and marching in place are the easiest places to start because they need no equipment and almost no learning. Step touches, low step-ups on a stair, and knee drives add variety. The best one is the move you'll do several times a week without pain.

### Can you lose weight with low impact cardio at home?
Low impact cardio burns calories and can be part of a weight loss plan, especially when it's paired with strength training and steady eating habits. Results vary from person to person. If weight loss is your goal, talk with your doctor about what's realistic for you.

### Is low impact cardio good for seniors?
Yes, for most older adults it's a good fit because it raises the heart rate without jumping. Use a chair for balance, start with short sessions, and check with your doctor first if you have heart, joint, or balance concerns.

### How long should a low impact cardio workout be?
Ten to 30 minutes is a good range. The CDC recommends 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, so 20 to 30 minutes most days covers it. Short sessions spread through the day count too.

### What low impact cardio can I do in a small space?
Marching in place, step touches, heel digs, knee drives, hamstring curls, and seated marches all fit in a space about the size of a yoga mat. A bottom stair adds step-ups without taking more room.
