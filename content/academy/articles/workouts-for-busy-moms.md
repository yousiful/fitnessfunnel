When you are a mom, the hardest part of working out is usually finding 20 quiet minutes between school runs, work, meals, laundry, and bedtime. So the routines below are short, need no equipment (a resistance band or a pair of light dumbbells is a bonus), and can be paused when someone needs a snack.

You will find three 15-minute workouts, a few 5-minute options for nap time, a sample week, and tips for fitting it all in.

**If you are postpartum, get your doctor's OK first.** That goes for any new routine after giving birth, and especially after a C-section, a difficult delivery, or if you notice leaking, pelvic heaviness, or a bulge down the middle of your belly when you sit up. A pelvic floor physical therapist can be very helpful here. The same goes for pregnancy, joint pain, or any health condition. Stop if anything hurts sharply.

## How much exercise you actually need

The CDC recommends adults aim for 150 minutes of moderate activity a week plus 2 days of muscle strengthening. That sounds like a lot until you break it up. Three 15-minute strength workouts plus a 15-minute walk with the stroller on four other days gets you there. Short bouts count. You do not have to do it all at once.

If 150 minutes feels far off right now, start with two of the workouts below each week and build from there.

## How to use these workouts

- Set a timer for each move: 40 seconds of work, 20 seconds of rest.
- Do the circuit 3 times. That is about 15 minutes with a quick warm-up.
- Go at a pace where you can talk in short sentences.
- Pick the easier or harder option for each move based on how you feel that day.

Warm up for 2 minutes before each one: march in place, roll your shoulders, swing your arms, and do a few slow bodyweight squats.

## Workout A: full body strength (15 minutes)

1. **Bodyweight squats.** Feet shoulder-width apart, sit your hips back like sitting into a chair, chest up, knees tracking over your toes. Stand up through your heels.
   - Easier: squat to a chair. Harder: hold a dumbbell, a full water jug, or your laughing toddler at your chest.
2. **Incline push-ups.** Hands on the kitchen counter or couch arm, body in a straight line, lower your chest toward your hands and press back.
   - Easier: higher surface. Harder: push-ups from your knees or toes on the floor.
3. **Glute bridges.** Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Press through your heels and lift your hips until your body makes a line from knees to shoulders. Squeeze, lower slowly.
   - Harder: one leg at a time, or a weight on your hips.
4. **Bent-over row.** Hinge forward at the hips with a flat back, holding dumbbells or water bottles. Pull your elbows back toward your ribs and lower.
   - No weights: use a resistance band under your feet, or a backpack with books.
5. **Dead bug.** Lie on your back, arms up, knees bent over hips. Gently press your lower back toward the floor and slowly lower the opposite arm and leg, then switch.
   - Easier: move only the legs, one heel tap at a time.

## Workout B: low-impact cardio (15 minutes)

No jumping, so it is quieter for napping kids and kinder on joints and the pelvic floor.

1. **Fast marching with arm punches.**
2. **Step-backs (reverse lunges).** Step one foot back, lower a little, return. Alternate. Hold a counter for balance if needed.
3. **Squat to calf raise.** Squat, stand, then rise onto your toes.
4. **Standing knee drives.** Arms overhead, pull one knee up toward your chest as you bring your arms down. Alternate quickly.
5. **Side steps.** Step wide to the side and back, staying low in a small squat.

## Workout C: legs, core, and posture (15 minutes)

Carrying a baby, a car seat, and a diaper bag puts a lot of load on your back and shoulders. This one helps.

1. **Split squats.** One foot forward, one back, lower straight down, then up. Switch legs halfway.
2. **Wall angels.** Stand with your back against a wall and slide your arms up and down like a slow snow angel, keeping your ribs down.
3. **Side-lying leg lifts.** Lie on your side and lift your top leg up and slightly back. Switch halfway.
4. **Bird dog.** On hands and knees, reach one arm forward and the opposite leg back, hold 2 seconds, switch.
5. **Plank on the counter or floor.** Hold a straight line from head to heels. Easier: hands on the counter. Harder: forearms on the floor.

## 5-minute nap-time options

On days when 15 minutes is not happening, do one of these. They still count.

- **Leg 5:** 1 minute each of squats, glute bridges, step-backs, calf raises, and wall sit.
- **Upper 5:** 1 minute each of counter push-ups, rows, wall angels, overhead press with water bottles, and bird dog.
- **Stroller walk:** a brisk 20 minutes around the block, kids included.

## A sample week

| Day | Workout | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Workout A | 15 min |
| Tuesday | Stroller or school-run walk | 15 to 20 min |
| Wednesday | Workout B | 15 min |
| Thursday | Walk or a 5-minute option | 5 to 20 min |
| Friday | Workout C | 15 min |
| Saturday | Family walk, bike ride, or park play | 20 to 30 min |
| Sunday | Rest and stretch | - |

Every 2 weeks, make it a little harder: add a fourth round, use the harder option, or swap water bottles for real dumbbells.

## Tips that help busy moms stick with it

- **Pick your slot and protect it.** Early morning before the house wakes up, nap time, or right after bedtime are the most common.
- **Include the kids.** Toddlers love copying squats and marching. Babies can lie on a mat next to you, or you can do bridges with them sitting on your hips (only once you are cleared and they have good head control).
- **Keep gear out.** A mat and a band on the living room floor are a reminder and remove a step.
- **Write it down.** Logging each session in a [free workout tracker](/app/) shows you the streak you are building, which helps on tired days.
- **Lower the bar on hard weeks.** A sick kid or a work deadline means you do the 5-minute version, not nothing. Our guide on [how to stay motivated to workout](academy:how-to-stay-motivated-to-workout) has more on this.
- **Sleep and food matter too.** Simple meals with protein and vegetables, water, and protecting sleep where you can all help your energy.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best workout for busy moms at home?
Short full-body strength circuits of 10 to 20 minutes, two or three times a week, plus daily walking. Squats, push-ups, bridges, rows, and core moves cover the whole body with little or no equipment. The best one is the one you can fit in consistently.

### Is a 15-minute workout enough?
Yes, for many people. Three 15-minute strength sessions plus regular walks can add up to the CDC's 150 minutes a week of moderate activity and 2 days of strength training. Make the workouts gradually harder over time to keep progressing.

### When can I start working out after having a baby?
It depends on your delivery and recovery, so get your doctor's OK first. Many women start with gentle walking and breathing and pelvic floor exercises in the early weeks, then add more after their postpartum checkup. Recovery after a C-section often takes longer.

### How can a mom with no time find time to exercise?
Look for small blocks: 15 minutes before the kids wake up, during nap time, or while dinner is in the oven. Five-minute circuits and stroller walks count. Planning your workout days at the start of each week also helps.

### Can I work out with my kids around?
Yes. Choose quieter, no-jump moves, keep the space clear of toys, and invite kids to join in. Many toddlers enjoy doing squats and marching with you, and it shows them that moving is a normal part of the day.
